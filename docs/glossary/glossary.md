# Glossary of Terms

## A

**Action** - In ROS 2, a communication pattern for long-running tasks that provides feedback during execution and returns a result upon completion, with support for goal preemption.

**Affordance** - The property of an object that defines how it can be interacted with, such as a handle for grasping or a button for pressing.

**Articulated Robot** - A robot with multiple rigid segments connected by joints, allowing for complex movement patterns.

**Autonomous System** - A system that can operate independently without direct human control, making decisions based on sensor input and internal algorithms.

## B

**Behavior Tree** - A hierarchical structure used in robotics and AI to organize and execute complex behaviors in a modular way.

**Biomechanics** - The study of the structure, function, and motion of biological systems, particularly relevant for humanoid robot design.

**Bridge** - In robotics, a software component that connects different frameworks or protocols, such as ROS to ROS 2 bridges.

**Brushless Motor** - A type of electric motor that uses electronic commutation instead of mechanical brushes, commonly used in robotics for precise control.

## C

**Cartesian Space** - The 3D space defined by X, Y, Z coordinates used to describe positions and orientations in robotics.

**Cascaded Control** - A control strategy where multiple controllers are arranged in series, with the output of one serving as the input to the next.

**CLIP (Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training)** - A vision-language model that learns visual concepts from natural language supervision.

**Collision Detection** - The computational process of determining when two or more objects come into contact with each other.

**Computer Vision** - A field of artificial intelligence that trains computers to interpret and understand the visual world.

**Control Loop** - A continuous cycle of sensing, processing, and actuating that enables a robot to maintain desired behavior.

**Convolutional Neural Network (CNN)** - A type of deep learning network particularly effective for image processing and computer vision tasks.

**Coordinate Frame** - A reference system used to define positions and orientations in space, essential for robot navigation and manipulation.

**Covariance** - In robotics, a measure of uncertainty in state estimation, commonly used in Kalman filters and SLAM algorithms.

## D

**Deep Learning** - A subset of machine learning that uses neural networks with multiple layers to learn complex patterns from data.

**Degrees of Freedom (DOF)** - The number of independent movements a robot or joint can make, typically referring to rotational or translational movements.

**Dexterous Manipulation** - The ability to perform fine, precise manipulation tasks using robotic hands or end-effectors.

**Digital Twin** - A virtual replica of a physical system that can be used for simulation, monitoring, and optimization.

**Differential Drive** - A common mobile robot configuration using two independently controlled wheels for movement and steering.

**Direct Kinematics** - The calculation of end-effector position and orientation based on known joint angles.

**Dynamic Movement Primitives (DMP)** - A method for representing and generating movements based on dynamical systems theory.

## E

**Embodied AI** - Artificial intelligence that is integrated with a physical body and interacts with the real world through sensors and actuators.

**Encoder** - A sensor that measures the position or speed of a rotating shaft, commonly used in robotic joints.

**End-Effector** - The tool or device at the end of a robotic arm that interacts with the environment.

**Episodic Memory** - In robotics and AI, the ability to store and recall specific experiences or events.

**Euclidean Distance** - The straight-line distance between two points in Euclidean space, commonly used in path planning.

**Extrinsic Calibration** - The process of determining the position and orientation of a sensor relative to a robot's coordinate system.

## F

**Feedback Control** - A control system that uses sensor measurements to adjust the system's behavior to achieve desired performance.

**Field of View (FOV)** - The extent of the observable world that can be seen by a camera or sensor at any given moment.

**Forward Kinematics** - The process of calculating the position and orientation of a robot's end-effector based on joint angles.

**Fused Deposition Modeling (FDM)** - A 3D printing technology commonly used for creating robot components and prototypes.

**Fuzzy Logic** - A form of many-valued logic that deals with reasoning that is approximate rather than fixed and exact.

## G

**Gazebo** - A 3D simulation environment for robotics that provides realistic physics simulation and sensor simulation.

**Generalized Coordinates** - A set of parameters that define the configuration of a mechanical system, often used in dynamics.

**Geometric Jacobian** - A matrix that relates joint velocities to end-effector velocities in robotics.

**Gripper** - A device used to grasp and hold objects, typically found at the end of a robotic arm.

**Ground Truth** - The accurate and reliable data used as a reference standard for evaluating the performance of algorithms.

**Gyroscope** - A sensor that measures angular velocity, commonly used in robot stabilization and orientation estimation.

## H

**Haptic Feedback** - The use of touch and motion to communicate information to a user, important for teleoperation.

**Heuristic** - A practical approach to problem-solving that is not guaranteed to be optimal but is sufficient for immediate goals.

**Human-Robot Interaction (HRI)** - The study of interactions between humans and robots, including communication and collaboration.

**Hybrid Systems** - Systems that combine continuous dynamics with discrete logical behavior, common in robotics.

## I

**Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU)** - A device that measures and reports a body's specific force, angular rate, and sometimes magnetic field.

**Inverse Kinematics** - The process of calculating required joint angles to achieve a desired end-effector position and orientation.

**Isaac Sim** - NVIDIA's simulation environment for robotics that provides photorealistic rendering and accurate physics simulation.

**Iterative Closest Point (ICP)** - An algorithm used to minimize the distance between points of two surfaces.

## J

**Jacobian Matrix** - A matrix of partial derivatives that describes the relationship between joint space and Cartesian space velocities.

**Joint Space** - The space defined by the joint angles of a robot, as opposed to Cartesian space.

**Jacobian Transpose Method** - A technique used in inverse kinematics to solve for joint velocities given desired Cartesian velocities.

## K

**Kalman Filter** - An algorithm that uses a series of measurements to estimate the state of a system, optimal for linear systems with Gaussian noise.

**Kinematics** - The study of motion without considering the forces that cause the motion.

**Kinetic Chain** - A series of rigid bodies connected by joints that transmit motion and force.

**Kubernetes** - A container orchestration platform that can be used for deploying and managing robotic applications at scale.

## L

**LIDAR (Light Detection and Ranging)** - A sensing technology that measures distances by illuminating targets with laser light.

**Localization** - The process of determining a robot's position and orientation within a known or unknown environment.

**Logistic Regression** - A statistical model used for binary classification problems in machine learning.

**Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM)** - A type of recurrent neural network designed to learn from sequences of data.

**Low-Level Control** - Direct control of actuators and sensors, typically operating at high frequencies with minimal processing.

## M

**Manipulator** - A robotic device designed to manipulate objects in the environment, typically an arm with a gripper.

**Machine Learning** - A field of artificial intelligence that enables systems to learn and improve from experience without explicit programming.

**Mapping** - The process of creating a representation of an environment based on sensor data.

**Middleware** - Software that provides common services and capabilities to applications beyond what's offered by the operating system.

**Mobile Robot** - A robot that can move around in its environment, as opposed to stationary robots.

**Monte Carlo Method** - A computational algorithm that relies on repeated random sampling to obtain numerical results.

## N

**Natural Language Processing (NLP)** - A field of AI focused on enabling computers to understand, interpret, and generate human language.

**Neural Network** - A computational model inspired by biological neural networks, used in machine learning applications.

**Newton-Euler Algorithm** - A method for computing inverse dynamics of robotic manipulators.

**Node** - In ROS/ROS 2, a process that performs computation, with multiple nodes working together to create complex systems.

## O

**Odometry** - The use of data from motion sensors to estimate change in position over time.

**OpenCV** - An open-source computer vision and machine learning software library.

**Operational Space** - The space in which a robot's end-effector operates, typically Cartesian space.

**Omnidirectional Drive** - A mobile robot configuration that can move in any direction without changing its orientation.

## P

**Path Planning** - The process of determining a route for a robot to follow from a start to a goal position.

**Perception System** - The component of a robot that processes sensor data to understand its environment.

**PID Controller** - A control loop feedback mechanism that uses proportional, integral, and derivative terms to control a system.

**Point Cloud** - A collection of data points in 3D space, typically generated by 3D scanners or LIDAR.

**Pose** - The position and orientation of an object in 3D space, typically represented by a 3D position and rotation.

**Proportional-Integral-Derivative (PID)** - A control algorithm that uses three terms to minimize error in a system.

## Q

**Q-Learning** - A model-free reinforcement learning algorithm that learns a policy for optimal action selection.

**Quaternion** - A mathematical construct used to represent rotations in 3D space without suffering from gimbal lock.

**Quality of Service (QoS)** - In ROS 2, a set of policies that define communication behavior between publishers and subscribers.

## R

**RANSAC (Random Sample Consensus)** - An iterative method to estimate parameters of a mathematical model from a set of observed data.

**Real-Time Operating System (RTOS)** - An operating system designed to process data as it comes in, typically without buffer delays.

**Reinforcement Learning** - A type of machine learning where an agent learns to make decisions by receiving rewards or penalties.

**Robot Operating System (ROS)** - A flexible framework for writing robot software, providing services like hardware abstraction and package management.

**Robotics Middleware** - Software that provides common services and capabilities to robot applications beyond what's offered by the OS.

**ROS 2** - The second generation of the Robot Operating System with improved real-time capabilities and security.

**RRT (Rapidly-exploring Random Tree)** - A path planning algorithm that builds a space-filling tree to find feasible paths.

## S

**Sensor Fusion** - The process of combining data from multiple sensors to improve accuracy and reliability.

**Service** - In ROS/ROS 2, a synchronous communication pattern for request-response interactions.

**SLAM (Simultaneous Localization and Mapping)** - The computational problem of constructing or updating a map while simultaneously keeping track of location.

**State Estimation** - The process of determining the current state of a system from noisy and incomplete measurements.

**Supervised Learning** - A type of machine learning where the model is trained on labeled data.

**Stereovision** - The process of extracting 3D information from 2D images using two or more cameras.

## T

**Task Planning** - The process of determining a sequence of actions to achieve a specified goal.

**Teaching Pendant** - A handheld device used to program and control industrial robots.

**Teleoperation** - The remote control of a robot by a human operator.

**TensorFlow** - An open-source machine learning framework developed by Google.

**Topic** - In ROS/ROS 2, a publish-subscribe communication pattern for asynchronous data exchange.

**Trajectory** - A path that includes timing information, specifying how a robot should move through space over time.

## U

**Universal Robot Description Format (URDF)** - An XML format for representing robot models in ROS.

**Unsupervised Learning** - A type of machine learning that finds patterns in data without labeled examples.

**USD (Universal Scene Description)** - NVIDIA's format for 3D scene representation used in Isaac Sim.

## V

**V-REP (CoppeliaSim)** - A robotics simulator that provides a complete development platform for robot control.

**Variational Autoencoder (VAE)** - A type of neural network used for generating new data similar to training data.

**Vision-Language Model (VLM)** - AI models that jointly process visual and textual information.

**VSLAM (Visual SLAM)** - Simultaneous localization and mapping using visual sensors.

**Voxel** - A volume element that represents a value on a regular 3D grid, used in 3D modeling and simulation.

## W

**Wheel Odometry** - Odometry derived from wheel encoders, commonly used for mobile robot localization.

**Workspace** - The space within which a robot can operate, limited by its kinematic and dynamic constraints.

**World Coordinate System** - A fixed reference frame used to define positions in a robot's environment.

## X

**Xacro** - An XML macro language for generating URDF files, allowing for parameterization and reuse.

## Y

**Yaw** - The rotation around the vertical (Z) axis of a robot or coordinate system.

## Z

**Zero Moment Point (ZMP)** - A concept in robotics and biomechanics used for dynamic stability analysis.

**Z-Buffer** - A component of 3D rendering that handles depth information to determine which objects are visible.
